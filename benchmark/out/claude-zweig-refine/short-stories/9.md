Marek Dolinsky lost on purpose for eleven years, and did it so well that no one ever proved it.

He'd resign a won ending two moves early, "missing" a fork any club player would see. He'd trade down into a drawn rook endgame, then push the wrong pawn at dead level. Arbiters never flagged it. Opponents thanked him for a hard-fought game. His rating hovered at 1900, exactly where a man plays who is trying and losing, nothing more.

The money came from a man named Pichler, who bet against him in club tournaments too small to watch closely, and who paid on time, in cash, in a manila envelope passed under the table at the coat check. It had started as one favor. It had never stopped being one favor.

Marek told himself he'd quit once he'd saved enough to leave the city. He told himself that at 1900 games, at 3800, at 6000. The number moved. The reason to stay never did.

The day it broke was an under-1800 open in a rented church hall, a girl in a school blazer who'd clearly memorized three openings and nothing past move fifteen. Marek reached for the losing move, a bishop retreat that would hand her a piece for nothing. His hand stopped over the board.

She was already smiling, the smile of someone who expects to be let off easy and is grateful in advance. He'd seen that smile a hundred times on the faces of men he was paid to lose to. He'd never seen it on someone who hadn't paid for it.

He played the fork instead. Knight takes, and her position came apart in four moves.

She didn't cry. She sat very straight and stared at the board like it had lied to her, then put out her hand and said good game. Marek shook it and felt something in his chest he didn't have a word for yet.

Pichler found him in the parking lot before he'd reached his car. He didn't ask why. He just named the figure, doubled to cover this one, and Marek nodded, because that part of the arrangement wasn't new.

What was new was that Marek drove home and, for the first time in eleven years, sat at his own board and played out the girl's fifteen memorized moves against himself, twice, hunting for what she should have done at move sixteen.

He found it before midnight. He wrote it on the back of the envelope Pichler still owed him.
